Through the real fit, with the classifier co-trained

The main experiment froze the classifier so that only the optimiser varied. TRL does not: it co-trains the classifier on the states the policy reaches. So the proposed patch was run through the public API on two datasets and both regimes, five seeds each. The shipped optimiser was given three passes; the group optimiser, one.

The conclusion survives co-training, with a smaller margin: clearly ahead on founders, level on credit, never clearly behind, in a third to a half of the time. With the patch's untouched defaults it is a little weaker, and slightly behind the shipped optimiser on credit in R1; the documentation says which two shared settings to change.

Group size

Mean-centred GRPO scores between 1.11 and 1.25 for every group size from 2 to 32: two episodes per sample already give a usable baseline. Canonical GRPO is poor at every size and worst at two, where a z-score can only be +1 or −1.

Four times the budget

TRL's default optimiser is not under-trained: four times the budget changes nothing. PPO keeps improving. Mean-centred GRPO does not hold its gains: part-way through it is level with PPO, and then it drops sharply, usually between two checkpoints, with no sign of over-fitting.

A diagnosis was registered before the rest of that experiment was read: the batch-wide scale the advantages are divided by shrinks as the policy converges, so the step never does. A one-line change to a scale that does not shrink cost nothing at the standard budget and helped a little at four times it (0.95 to 1.12), but 6 of 12 runs still collapsed. The diagnosis is at most part of the story.
